# ScrollLibrary Partner Outreach Playbook v1

Status: Commercial execution baseline
Date: 2026-09-12

## Objective

Build a partner network that gives ScrollLibrary Press professional reach into bookshops, libraries, universities and regional markets while ScrollLibrary retains control of publication identity, ISBN provenance, validation, rights and technology.

No outreach should imply that ScrollLibrary needs another publisher's permission to exist. The proposition is collaboration around distribution, market access, local expertise and infrastructure.

## Core partner pitch

Use this positioning consistently:

> ScrollLibrary is publishing and knowledge-trust infrastructure for authors, publishers, libraries and institutions. ScrollLibrary Press is our publishing imprint. We are building a standards-based publication pipeline around verified rights, edition identity, ISBN provenance, validation, trade/library metadata, public verification and intelligent reading. We are looking for partners who strengthen local production, distribution, institutional access and specialist publishing expertise while preserving clear ownership and accountability.

Do not lead with "AI-generated books". AI is an enabling technology, not the commercial identity.

Subject: Pilot proposal — validated digital and print publications from ScrollLibrary

Hello,

ScrollLibrary is developing publishing and library infrastructure designed to make the provenance and status of a publication transparent: who published it, which edition is being read, what rights attach to it, what validation it has passed and whether it has been corrected or superseded.

We are preparing a small institutional pilot and would value feedback from a library/university partner on acquisition metadata, validation information, accessibility, digital access and AI-assisted discovery grounded in licensed collections.

The pilot would be deliberately small and evidence-driven rather than a request for broad catalogue adoption.

## First-call questions

### For printers

- What print methods, trim sizes, bindings and paper stocks are supported?
- Minimum economic quantity for offset vs digital?
- Unit economics at 50 / 100 / 500 / 1,000 copies?
- Can they fulfil single-copy or small-batch orders?
- Quality-control process and replacement policy?
- Storage and pick/pack capability?
- Can they ship directly to booksellers or institutions?
- Who owns production files and plates?
- How are defective copies handled?

### For distributors / wholesalers

- Publisher onboarding requirements?
- Required ONIX version/metadata fields?
- ISBN/GTIN requirements?
- Stock vs POD options?
- Wholesale discount expectations?
- Returns options and costs?
- Territory coverage?
- Library and education channels?
- Payment cycle?
- Metadata update latency?
- Sales/inventory reporting?
- Who controls retail availability status?

### For publishers

- Catalogue and subject strengths?
- Editorial/production capabilities?
- Distribution territories?
- Institutional relationships?
- Co-publishing models?
- Rights split and term?
- Translation/subsidiary rights expectations?
- ISBN ownership by edition?
- Who controls metadata?
- Who controls validated master files?
- Exit and reversion rights?
- Interest in using ScrollLibrary infrastructure for their own catalogue?

### For libraries / universities

- MARC/MARCXML requirements?
- Acquisition workflow and preferred suppliers?
- Ebook licensing expectations?
- Authentication standards?
- Accessibility requirements?
- Preservation requirements?
- Correction/retraction handling?
- Validation information librarians actually value?
- AI-search policy constraints?
- Usage statistics required?
- Data privacy requirements?

## Deal guardrails

Reject or renegotiate any arrangement that:

- requires false publisher/ISBN metadata;
- allows a partner to alter a validated edition silently;
- grants perpetual broad rights without adequate value;
- transfers ScrollLibrary platform IP as a condition of distribution;
- makes validation outcomes commercially purchasable;
- prevents export of ScrollLibrary's own publication/metadata records;
- bundles territories unnecessarily;
- obscures returns, warehousing or fulfilment liability;
- prevents transparent correction/retraction history.

## Execution rule

Every partner conversation must end in one of four explicit states:

- rejected / not a fit;
- information required;
- pilot candidate;
- commercial negotiation.

Record evidence, owner, next action and date. Do not allow partnership discussions to become indefinite relationship-building without measurable progression.
