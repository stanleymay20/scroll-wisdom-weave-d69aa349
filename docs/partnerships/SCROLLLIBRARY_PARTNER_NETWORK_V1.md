# ScrollLibrary Partner Network v1

Status: Strategy baseline
Owner: ScrollLibrary / ScrollLibrary Press
Date: 2026-09-12

## Purpose

ScrollLibrary should operate as an independent publishing and knowledge-infrastructure platform while using external partners for reach, logistics, local market access, specialist review and institutional adoption. Partnerships are leverage, not dependency.

ScrollLibrary Press remains capable of publishing its own titles directly. Partners are selected where they improve distribution, printing economics, local credibility, institutional access or market speed.

## Core operating model

ScrollLibrary owns the trust and publishing control plane:

- author and publisher identity
- rights and provenance records
- ISBN governance for ScrollLibrary Press editions
- immutable publication and edition identity
- validation passports
- metadata generation and quality controls
- ONIX/MARC/library export readiness
- digital formats and accessibility metadata
- correction, supersession and retraction history
- public verification

Partners may provide:

- physical printing
- warehousing and fulfilment
- wholesale distribution
- bookshop access
- library procurement access
- local-language production
- specialist editorial and subject review
- co-publishing
- territorial sales representation
- institutional implementation

## Partner classes

### 1. Printing partners

Use for regional short runs, offset production, specialist formats and local fulfilment.

Preferred structure:

- Publisher: ScrollLibrary Press
- Printer: named production partner
- Distributor: separate where applicable

A printer does not become publisher of record merely by manufacturing the book.

### 2. Wholesale and distribution partners

Priority targets:

- Ingram / IngramSpark for broad international print and ebook distribution
- Gardners for UK, Europe and institutional/retail reach
- Libri / BoD infrastructure for German-language-region availability and print-on-demand
- VLB/MVB for German trade metadata and discoverability

Objective: make ScrollLibrary Press titles discoverable and orderable through normal trade channels rather than requiring booksellers to understand a bespoke supply chain.

### 3. Publisher and co-publisher partners

Use selectively where an established publisher contributes meaningful local or specialist advantage.

Initial Ghana ecosystem targets include:

- Ghana Publishers Association as industry-entry and relationship layer
- Afram Publications for educational and Ghanaian literature expertise
- Sub-Saharan Publishers for African publishing and international-facing catalogue experience
- other Ghana Book Development Council-listed publishers/booksellers after due diligence

Co-publishing is optional. ScrollLibrary should not surrender its technology, validation or edition-control layer merely to gain a logo.

### 4. Bookshop partners

Use for stocking, launch events, local visibility, author events, curated displays and demand evidence.

Trade-readiness requirements should include:

- valid format-specific ISBN
- complete ONIX-quality metadata
- professional cover/interior
- retail and wholesale pricing
- availability and lead time
- returns policy where commercially appropriate
- territorial rights
- subject classification
- distributor/wholesaler route
- sample material and marketing assets

### 5. Library and university partners

Treat libraries as both customers and strategic infrastructure partners.

Offer:

- validated print and digital collections
- MARC-ready metadata
- edition and correction history
- rights/licensing records
- accessibility metadata
- institutional access controls
- grounded AI search across licensed collections
- source/claim transparency
- usage analytics with privacy controls
- collection-level validation policies

### 6. Institutional white-label partners

Long-term product: ScrollLibrary Institutional Library OS.

Institution receives its own branded portal while ScrollLibrary supplies the underlying catalogue, rights, validation, search, reading, AI and preservation infrastructure.

Possible customer classes:

- universities
- public libraries
- ministries and government agencies
- research institutions
- schools
- churches and theological institutions
- cultural and traditional institutions
- publishers with large backlists

## Partnership deal types

Use five standard deal structures.

1. **Service supplier** — printer, formatter, fulfilment provider. No publishing control.
2. **Distributor/wholesaler** — trade access and logistics. ScrollLibrary remains publisher.
3. **Territorial partner** — local sales/representation rights within defined territory and term.
4. **Co-publisher** — shared commercial publication with explicit ownership, metadata and rights responsibilities.
5. **Platform partner** — publisher/library/institution uses ScrollLibrary technology for its own catalogue.

Every agreement should specify ownership of ISBN, files, metadata, customer data, territorial rights, digital rights, translation rights, returns, inventory, validation records and termination/export rights.

## Non-negotiable control boundaries

For a ScrollLibrary Press-controlled edition:

- ISBN registrant/publisher identity must remain factually correct.
- No partner may reuse an ISBN across a materially different edition or format.
- A partner may not silently alter a validated publication file.
- A changed edition must receive a new immutable publication state and validation status as required.
- Rights and provenance evidence must remain auditable.
- Validation cannot be purchased as an automatic positive result.
- Commercial partnership must not override independent-review separation.
- ScrollLibrary must retain exportability of its own metadata and publication records.

## First pilot offer

Create a tightly controlled 3-5 title ScrollLibrary Press pilot rather than launching hundreds of titles.

Each pilot title should have:

- format-specific ISBN
- immutable publication identity
- complete rights record
- Validation Passport
- polished print and EPUB editions
- ONIX-ready metadata
- library metadata package
- wholesale terms
- international and regional distribution route
- public verification page

Success evidence:

- title discoverable in expected trade databases
- bookshop order completes successfully
- library order completes successfully
- metadata matches physical/digital edition
- no ISBN drift
- no rights ambiguity
- fulfilment SLA observed
- return/cancellation path tested

## 90-day commercial programme

### Days 1-30: trade foundation

- finish ISBN/staging convergence and production verification
- ship Validation Passport V1
- implement Trade Readiness and Library Readiness specifications
- produce partner-facing one-page capability statement
- prepare publisher/distributor due-diligence questionnaire
- prepare sample ONIX and library metadata package
- select first 3-5 controlled pilot books

### Days 31-60: partner conversations

- approach GPA and selected Ghana publishers
- approach one Ghana printing/fulfilment partner
- begin Ingram production/distribution pilot
- open Gardners distribution conversation
- evaluate Libri/BoD onboarding
- register/prepare German trade metadata through VLB where applicable
- approach 3-5 university/public-library pilot candidates

### Days 61-90: prove transactions

- complete at least one bookshop order through wholesale channel
- complete at least one library/institutional acquisition
- run one Ghana local-print fulfilment test
- run one Germany/EU fulfilment test
- measure delivery time, discount, returns, print quality and metadata correctness
- collect partner feedback and convert it into product requirements

## Partner scorecard

Score every potential partner on:

- channel reach
- reputation
- technical integration capability
- metadata standards
- fulfilment reliability
- print quality
- library/bookshop penetration
- territorial strength
- commercial margin
- rights discipline
- data portability
- willingness to support small/independent publishers
- strategic conflict risk

Do not select partners solely on prestige.

## Positioning

ScrollLibrary should not present itself as "AI self-publishing".

Preferred positioning:

> ScrollLibrary is publishing and knowledge-trust infrastructure for authors, publishers, libraries and institutions.

ScrollLibrary Press is the publishing imprint. The platform underneath provides verifiable edition identity, rights/provenance, validation, metadata, distribution readiness and intelligent reading infrastructure.

## Strategic objective

The end state is a network in which a creator can publish through ScrollLibrary Press or an external publisher can use ScrollLibrary infrastructure, and the resulting title can move through recognised wholesalers, bookshops, libraries and institutions while preserving a verifiable chain of publication identity, rights and evidence.

The moat is not printing. It is the trusted control plane connecting creation, validation, publication identity, metadata, distribution and institutional reading.
